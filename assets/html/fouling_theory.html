Stochastic fouling theory

One fouling curve, four mechanisms

An asymptotic fouling curve is usually read as the signature of shear removal. It is not. Erosion, detachment, sloughing and surface saturation all draw exactly the same mean curve. What tells them apart is the scatter around it.

Same curve, different mechanism

Pick a mechanism. The black line, the mean fouling resistance, does not move. The individual records and the band they fill do.

Fitting the curve returns a plateau and a time constant, and nothing else. A Kern–Seaton fit is not evidence of shear removal.

The maths

With deposition at mean rate m and removal rate ψ, every mechanism here gives E[R(t)] = R∞(1 − e−ψt), with R∞ = m/ψ. The plateau variance differs: s²/2ψ for continuous erosion, s²/ψ for per-element detachment, s²/((2−η)ψ) + ηR∞²/(2−η) for sloughing of a fraction η, and zero for saturation, whose variance ℓR∞θ(1−θ) peaks at half coverage. Simulated with m = 1, c = 1, ψ = 0.2, η = 0.5, as in Fig. 3 of the paper.

Can you measure the deposition law?

Deposition laws with the same mean and variance differ only in the skewness of their increments. Measurement noise hides it at short windows, and averaging erases it at long ones. Set your instrument and see how long a record you would need.

The record needed grows as the fourth power of the measurement noise. Improving the instrument helps far more than waiting.

The maths

With relative conductance error ε and relative conductance change per period ζ, the noise-to-signal ratio is ρ = (2/c²)(ε/ζ)². Over a window of k periods the skewness is reduced to k/(k+ρ)3/2 of its one-period value, which peaks at k = 2ρ. Separating the two laws at two standard errors needs N ≈ 324 ρ²/c² = 1296 (ε/ζ)⁴/c⁶ sampling periods, here of three days each.

What this means in practice

  • A fitted fouling curve gives a net rate and a relaxation time. It does not identify the mechanism.
  • To identify the removal mechanism, watch the scatter on the plateau. Sloughing keeps a large variance even when deposition is smooth.
  • The deposition law is out of reach for a thermally reconstructed resistance at realistic accuracy.
